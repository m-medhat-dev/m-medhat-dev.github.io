Prologue · 2007 to 2013 · The Spark

The Three Lines That Changed Everything

Back in university in 2007, moving a single element across a browser screen took two hundred lines of fragile JavaScript that broke in every other browser. The web still felt rigid and lifeless, so after graduating that year I stepped away and spent six years teaching programming in schools.

Then one afternoon in 2013, I pulled open the side menu of the Android Play Store on my phone. Three horizontal lines smoothly rotated and morphed into a crisp arrow right under my thumb. No Flash. No video file. Just pure, fluid code responding to human touch.

That single animation changed my career. I closed out my teaching years, moved fully into web development, opened Adobe Brackets with its live preview, and dove headfirst into HTML5, CSS3, and jQuery. The web had come alive, and I wanted to build all of it.

Case study · Zimam (Imtithal) · Dec 2025 to Jun 2026

The Backend Behind a 1M+ User Platform

Three backend systems I built for a national workforce compliance platform

At Zimam I owned the whole RMS platform and parts of the OMS, and worked directly with the PM and the GM on release planning. Most of the work users never see: keeping two platforms in agreement, producing legal documents people sign, and storing files safely across clouds. Here is how each piece works.

01

Two-way RMS / OMS integration

Employees, roles and credentials lived in two platforms that had to agree. I built a two-way integration that syncs employees and propagates roles and credentials, plus a dedicated sync command to run a full sync on demand.

Why a dedicated command, not only automatic sync: day-to-day changes flow automatically, but when the two platforms drift apart (after an outage, a bulk import or a new environment), one command brings them back in line without manual fixes.

  1. RMS
  2. Employee sync
  3. Roles & credentials
  4. OMS

Plus a dedicated sync command

02

DOCX to PDF legal documents

Four legal document types (Contract, NDA, Custody and Custody Handover) are generated from DOCX templates with template variables, digital signatures with image cropping and Hijri dates, then converted to PDF with unoserver and cached.

Why DOCX templates: the administration revised the legal wording many times a day. Keeping that wording in Word let them change it themselves, and unoserver renders the exact layout to PDF, so a wording change never needed a code change. Caching avoids re-rendering the same document.

  1. DOCX template
  2. Variables, signature, Hijri date
  3. unoserver
  4. PDF, cached

4 document types

03

One storage service, three backends

A unified storage service over local disk, Google Cloud Storage and AWS S3. Files are served through signed URLs and a secure file controller, with backward compatibility for legacy file paths.

Why one storage service: the app talks to one interface, so local disk, GCS or S3 is a configuration choice, not code. Signed URLs keep private files off public links, and legacy path support let old files keep working without a migration.

  1. Storage service
  2. Local / GCS / S3
  3. Signed URL
  4. Secure file controller

Backward compatible with legacy paths

Chapter 4 · 2023 · 2025 · From a Café Napkin to the Factory Floor

A Café Conversation, Drones, and the Factory Floor

The birth of SmartX: from coffee shop napkin to ITI embedded systems to industrial IIoT SaaS

In 2023, I was sitting in a café with an electrical engineer friend talking about how to build custom drones. In the middle of our conversation, he stopped me and asked a simple question:

"Can you build me a small web app I can open on my phone to see the Andon buttons attached to my factory machines, so I know immediately which machine stopped and which line needs raw materials?"

I built that small system for him, my first SmartX prototype. Seeing the operators and engineers on his factory floor light up when they used it sparked a much bigger question in my mind: How far could this go?

I dove into deep R&D and discovered the world of Industrial IoT (IIoT). My friend's younger brother, a quality engineer, taught me the mathematics and factory discipline behind OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness). I realized very few companies in our region were solving this properly, and managing real production lines inside factories became my dream.

To build it right, I went back to the classroom as a student: I enrolled in the Embedded Systems program at ITI in Egypt, learned C++ and hardware interfacing from the ground up, built dozens of IoT hardware prototypes, and created SmartX Solutions solo: an end-to-end industrial platform combining custom ESP32 SmartX Gateways, MQTT real-time telemetry, multi-tenant Laravel 12 + Vue 3 + TypeScript cloud dashboards, and instant Andon escalation. I worked on SmartX full-time from July to December 2025.

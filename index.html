Prologue · 2007 to 2013 · The Spark

The Three Lines That Changed Everything

Back in university in 2007, making even a simple JavaScript animation work across every browser was a headache. The web felt slow and boring to me back then, so after graduating I took a different path and spent six years teaching programming.

Then one day in 2013, I opened the Android Play Store on my phone and noticed something that completely caught me off guard. That small detail showed me how much the web had evolved. I put teaching aside and went all-in on modern web development: HTML5, CSS3, and jQuery, coding in Adobe Brackets with its live preview.

Case study · Zimam (Imtithal) · Jul 2025 to Jul 2026

The Backend Behind a 1M+ User Platform

Three backend systems I built for a national workforce compliance platform

At Zimam I owned the whole RMS platform and parts of the OMS, and worked directly with the PM and the GM on release planning. Most of the work users never see: keeping two platforms in agreement, producing legal documents people sign, and storing files safely across clouds. Here is how each piece works.

01

Two-way RMS / OMS integration

Employees, roles and credentials lived in two platforms that had to agree. I built a two-way integration that syncs employees and propagates roles and credentials, plus a dedicated sync command to run a full sync on demand.

Why a dedicated command, not only automatic sync: day-to-day changes flow automatically, but when the two platforms drift apart (after an outage, a bulk import or a new environment), one command brings them back in line without manual fixes.

  1. RMS
  2. Employee sync
  3. Roles & credentials
  4. OMS

Plus a dedicated sync command

02

DOCX to PDF legal documents

Four legal document types (Contract, NDA, Custody and Custody Handover) are generated from DOCX templates with template variables, digital signatures with image cropping and Hijri dates, then converted to PDF with unoserver and cached.

Why DOCX templates: the administration revised the legal wording many times a day. Keeping that wording in Word let them change it themselves, and unoserver renders the exact layout to PDF, so a wording change never needed a code change. Caching avoids re-rendering the same document.

  1. DOCX template
  2. Variables, signature, Hijri date
  3. unoserver
  4. PDF, cached

4 document types

03

One storage service, three backends

A unified storage service over local disk, Google Cloud Storage and AWS S3. Files are served through signed URLs and a secure file controller, with backward compatibility for legacy file paths.

Why one storage service: the app talks to one interface, so local disk, GCS or S3 is a configuration choice, not code. Signed URLs keep private files off public links, and legacy path support let old files keep working without a migration.

  1. Storage service
  2. Local / GCS / S3
  3. Signed URL
  4. Secure file controller

Backward compatible with legacy paths

Chapter 4 · 2023 to 2026 · BDS (Business Development Services) · Multi-Tenant ERP & Industrial IoT

A Café Conversation, Drones, and the Factory Floor

Building BDS's Next.js & Prisma multi-country ERP tenancy, AI vision, and the SmartX IIoT platform

Three years ago, I was sitting in a café with a friend from BDS (Business Development Services) talking about drones and how to build them at different scales. Halfway through our conversation, he stopped me and asked if I could build a simple mobile web app to monitor the Andon buttons attached to their factory machines, so the team could see right away which machine stopped and which line needed raw materials.

I built that first prototype for BDS, and the operators on the factory floor loved it. That opened the door to a much larger engineering scope with BDS across both their enterprise ERP and their factory-floor operations.

On the enterprise side, I re-architected BDS's core Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, and Prisma 7 ERP system into a multi-tenant platform with completely isolated PostgreSQL databases and automated daily backup pipelines supporting branches across Egypt and Saudi Arabia. I engineered background calculation workers (pg-boss and Decimal.js) across 450k+ ledger rows and wrote 633 automated Vitest unit and database-backed integration tests to stabilize critical financial and import workflows.

In parallel, I researched Industrial IoT (IIoT) and OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness), and completed the Embedded Systems track at ITI in Egypt so I could engineer the hardware-to-cloud layer properly. Before you can add smart analytics or AI to a business, the basic data foundation has to exist. Going directly into real operations to find out what is missing and build the system from scratch is what tech companies today call Forward-Deployed Engineering.

For BDS, I built the complete SmartX Industrial IoT ecosystem from the ground up: custom ESP32 gateways in C++, AI vision camera integration, real-time MQTT and WebSocket telemetry, a NestJS + TypeScript escalation engine, and multi-tenant cloud dashboards. With both the multi-tenant ERP and the SmartX platform delivered and running in production for BDS, I am now ready for my next long-term Senior Software Engineering role.
